archetypesThree users, one spine

Not three journeys — one journey at three depths. Every milestone has a single minimum-to-aha action that is the default for everyone. Initial does only that; Learning adds a guided choice or two; Advanced expands the same step into full control. Depth is progressive disclosure, never a fork.

who we are designing for

The three archetypes

Initial
“Just show me it works.”
KnowsFirst contact — may not know what a knowledge graph is.
Success =Sees a grounded, cited answer — fast.
Config toleranceZero. Defaults only.
Learning
“Help me build mine, properly.”
KnowsRamping — building their first real graph from own docs.
Success =A graph they trust, and understand how it was made.
Config toleranceA few meaningful choices, with recommended defaults.
Advanced
“Give me the levers.”
KnowsEngineer / operator. Own infra, own models, agents.
Success =Full control — and it still ran on sane defaults.
Config toleranceHigh — wants levers, not hand-holding.

The principle · ruthless simplicity

The mandatory spine is just three steps — explore → ingest → connect. That is the shortest path to a live graph, and it is the default everyone is dropped onto. Make-ready (M2) and own-stack (M3) are opt-in depth: real value for Learning and Advanced, never required to reach an aha.

Design consequence: build the spine columns first and make them flawless — they carry every archetype's aha. Everything in the Learning / Advanced columns is progressive disclosure layered onto the same screen, gated behind an “Advanced” reveal or reachable later from Graph home. No archetype ever sees a different journey.
