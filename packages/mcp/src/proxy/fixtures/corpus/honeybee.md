# The Honey Bee (public-domain reference text)

The western honey bee, Apis mellifera, lives in colonies that may contain tens of thousands of
individuals. A colony has three castes: a single reproductive queen, many sterile female workers,
and seasonal males called drones.

Worker bees perform almost every task in the hive. They forage for nectar and pollen, build the
wax comb, feed the larvae, and defend the colony. A forager that has found a good source of food
returns to the hive and performs a "waggle dance" that communicates the direction and distance of
the food to other workers.

The queen's primary role is to lay eggs; a healthy queen can lay more than a thousand eggs in a
single day. Drones do not gather food and have no sting; their sole function is to mate with a
queen.
