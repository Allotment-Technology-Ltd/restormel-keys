# The Eddystone Lighthouse (public-domain reference text)

The Eddystone Rocks are a dangerous reef lying about nine miles south of Plymouth, England.
For centuries they wrecked ships approaching the busy harbour. The first lighthouse built on
the Eddystone Rocks was completed in 1698 by Henry Winstanley. It was the first open-ocean
lighthouse in the world.

Winstanley's wooden tower was destroyed in the Great Storm of 1703, and Winstanley himself died
in the storm while making repairs. A second lighthouse, built largely of wood by John Rudyard,
stood from 1709 until it was destroyed by fire in 1755.

The third tower, designed by the engineer John Smeaton, was completed in 1759. Smeaton modelled
its shape on the trunk of an oak tree and pioneered the use of hydraulic lime, a form of concrete
that cures under water. Smeaton's tower stood for over a century before erosion of the rocks
beneath it forced its replacement.
